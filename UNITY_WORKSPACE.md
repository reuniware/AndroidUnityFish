# Unity Android workspace handoff

Last updated: September 4, 2026

## Purpose

This document is the handoff reference for the Unity Android project. Read it before changing the project or starting a new work session.

The current demo is **Aqua Flow**: a portrait-locked, dependency-free water simulation. It renders a deep animated pool with propagating waves, rain drops, rising bubbles, drifting light rays and particles, touch ripples, animated caustic lighting, subtle accelerometer-driven flow, and four 3D fish — a rigged Emperor Angelfish plus three Low Poly companions that swim as a little school. You can feed the fish, tap to startle them, and pause the aquarium from a corner button.

## Current demo: Aqua Flow

`Assets/HelloAndroid.cs` contains the complete demo using Unity's built-in `OnGUI` API and no scene-authored UI.

### Visible result

- Full-screen deep blue/turquoise water surface
- Smooth bilinear-rendered procedural texture
- Animated wave highlights and caustic-like patterns
- Small falling rain drops that create new wave sources
- Rising translucent bubbles (denser near the top)
- Drifting light rays from the surface and slow floating particles for depth
- Darker gradient toward the floor with a faint caustic shimmer at the bottom
- Soft moving shadows under each fish so they look grounded in the water
- A realistic, rigged, animated Emperor Angelfish swimming over the water
- Three Low Poly fish companions with independent continuous swim motion
- The fish are loaded from `Resources`, auto-oriented and auto-scaled at runtime
- A pause button (two bars) in the top-right corner
- Compact `AQUA FLOW` title and simulation subtitle
- Touch/click instruction at the bottom

### Interaction

- Touch the water to create a strong ripple at the touched position **and drop a food pellet**.
- Pellet sinks slowly toward the floor; hungry fish within reach swim over and eat it (a warm flash marks the bite).
- A tap close to a fish startles it: it darts away for a moment instead of feeding.
- Dragging creates a sequence of smaller ripples.
- Tapping the two-bar pause button freezes the simulation and dims the screen; tapping the overlay resumes it.
- Resuming after a pause is the intended **interstitial-ad trigger point** (`OnResumeFromPause` logs `ADSPOT`); wiring AdMob there is documented but not yet done.
- On a development machine, mouse input is supported inside the Unity Editor (click = ripple + food).
- Gently tilting the phone biases the simulated flow using the accelerometer.
- The fish swims continuously, turns around near the screen edges, and gently responds to tilt-driven flow.
- Each fish has independent motion; companions use separate swim bands and no longer stop to converge on a leader.
- The fish layer is rendered by a disabled transparent perspective camera during `LateUpdate` into a ping-pong pair of filtered render textures; `OnGUI` only composites the completed front frame over the water.
- The phone remains locked in portrait mode.

### Simulation model

- Grid resolution: `112 x 192` cells.
- Each cell stores wave height and velocity.
- Every frame, each interior cell receives a restoring force based on the average of its four neighbors.
- Wave velocity is damped to keep the simulation stable.
- Border cells absorb energy to avoid endless reflections at the texture edge.
- Rain and touch events inject localized height and velocity impulses.
- The rendered color combines depth, wave height, local slope, moving light, and animated caustic patterns.
- Rendering is done into a runtime `Texture2D` using bilinear filtering.
- Bubble, fish halo, and rain overlays use generated radial glow textures.
- A short synthesized tone plays for rain impacts.

### Emperor Angelfish integration

- Prefab: `Assets/Mikhail Nesterov/Emperor Angelfish/Prefab/EmperorAngelfish_swim1.prefab`, copied to `Assets/Resources/` for runtime loading.
- The rigged model (`EmperorAngelfish.fbx`) plays its looping `Swim1` animation via the included Animator controller.
- At startup the script detects the fish's head/tail and dorsal/ventral axes from named bones (`Jaw`/`Head`/`Eye`, `TailFin`, `FinDorsal`, `FinPelvic`/`FinAnal`), aligns the nose to `+X`, and centers the model on its pivot.
- The fish is auto-scaled to roughly 44% of the reference camera view width (height-capped) using the animated pose extents measured during the first ~1.6 seconds.
- The fit scale is applied on the outer `fishRoot` so the centering offset and the scale stay consistent (the model is ~36 units long in its own space).
- The fish camera is **perspective** (FOV ~17.6°) so the fish can swim closer to / farther from the camera and its apparent size changes with depth (`EmperorDepthFar`/`EmperorDepthNear` in world Z).
- Placement is **depth-aware** (`PlaceFishAt`): a normalized fish position means "fraction across the visible area at that depth", so a far fish near the left/right edge is never sliced by the smaller far-plane frustum. The fish body is kept fully inside the view with a hairline pad; when a free-swimming fish presses the edge it turns around (the bounce is disabled while chasing food or fleeing so it does not vibrate).
- Movement is fish-like: smooth yaw turns with banking roll into the turn, nose pitch with the vertical bob, independent continuous swimming, occasional glides, random mid-water heading changes, and depth excursions.
- Every fish is separated in composited screen space by `ApplyEmperorSeparation` using projected rectangular body bounds and repeated shallowest-penetration corrections, so fish cannot overlap while cruising.
- The fish camera is disabled and rendered manually from `LateUpdate` after animation/transform updates. It renders into a back texture, then atomically swaps the ping-pong front/back references; `OnGUI` never samples the texture currently being rendered. HDR, MSAA, occlusion culling, and skinned motion vectors are disabled for this isolated Android pass.
- Turns are smooth (yaw sweeps up to 180°) instead of an instant flip; the fish is rendered on layer 8 with its own perspective camera, directional light, and ambient fill.
- The Emperor Angelfish asset materials target the URP Lit shader. `BuildAndroidTest` converts them to the built-in Standard shader at build time (shader reference swap plus keyword/gloss fixes) so the fish renders under the built-in render pipeline.

### Low Poly Fish companions

- Three static-mesh fish from the **Floreswa Low Poly Fish** asset (`Assets/Floreswa`) also swim in the pool: `fish01`, `fish02`, `fish03` (copied to `Assets/Resources/`). Their materials are embedded in each FBX and already use the built-in Standard shader.
- They face `-Z` in model space (probe-verified); the runtime rotates them to swim nose-first (`+X`) and centers them on their pivot.
- Each companion has its own position/depth/yaw state and independent continuous fish-like motion (glides, random turns, banking, depth excursions). They are parented under a neutral `AquaFlow_SimpleFish` root — never under the emperor's `fishRoot`, which carries the emperor's own fit scale and position.
- The three companions use distinct vertical swim bands and independent headings, so the separation system does not pin them in a follower/leader feedback loop. All three (and the emperor) break formation to chase a food pellet or flee a nearby tap.
- `ApplyEmperorSeparation` keeps all four fish from overlapping in the composited screen view using projected rectangular body bounds. It runs after movement and resolves only the shallowest penetration, preserving forward swimming.

### Feeding, reactions, and pause UI

- Food pellets are small emissive spheres built **by hand** (`BuildSphereMesh`) because IL2CPP strips `SphereCollider`, which `GameObject.CreatePrimitive` needs; a warm glow dot is also drawn over each pellet so it stays visible.
- Pellets live up to `MaxPellets` (8) and expire after ~9 s; they sink toward the floor (`PelletSinkSpeed`) with a gentle sway.
- The emperor notices food within `PelletChaseRadius` (0.40 in normalized space, fish only chase when hungry — a ~7 s cooldown after eating); companions use a slightly smaller radius and shorter cooldown.
- All touch handling is done from `Input.touches` in `Update`. IMGUI `GUI.Button` is used only for drawing, because GUI buttons proved unreliable for click handling on the device — the pause toggle and the "tap to resume" overlay are manual rectangle hit-tests (`HandlePauseInput`).
- The `ADSPOT` log line in `OnResumeFromPause()` is the natural place for a future AdMob interstitial (load it while paused, show it on resume). No ad SDK is installed.

## Android toolchain

`Assets/Editor/BuildAndroidTest.cs` selects the Unity 6 Android tools explicitly. The toolchain root is resolved from the user's local application data folder at build time:

```csharp
var localAppData = Environment.GetFolderPath(Environment.SpecialFolder.LocalApplicationData);
var toolchainRoot = Path.Combine(localAppData, "Unity", "AndroidToolchain-6000.6.0f1");
AndroidExternalToolsSettings.jdkRootPath = Path.Combine(toolchainRoot, "OpenJDK");
AndroidExternalToolsSettings.sdkRootPath = Path.Combine(toolchainRoot, "SDK");
AndroidExternalToolsSettings.ndkRootPath = Path.Combine(toolchainRoot, "NDK");
```

The build also adds the built-in Standard shader to the always-included shader list so materials created or converted at runtime are never stripped from the player.

## Portrait lock

The app intentionally stays in upright portrait. This is configured in `ProjectSettings/ProjectSettings.asset` and reinforced in `HelloAndroid.Awake()` and `EnsurePortraitScreen()`:

- `defaultScreenOrientation: 0` (portrait)
- `allowedAutorotateToPortrait: 0`
- `allowedAutorotateToPortraitUpsideDown: 0`
- `allowedAutorotateToLandscapeRight: 0`
- `allowedAutorotateToLandscapeLeft: 0`
- `useOSAutorotation: 0`
- Runtime `Screen.orientation` is forced to `Portrait`

Android's global automatic-rotation preference cannot rotate this application. Do not add orientation remapping or 180-degree special cases unless multi-orientation support is intentionally reintroduced.

The project originally used Unity `2022.3.62f3` and has been migrated to Unity `6000.6.0f1`. Unity 6 regenerated the local `Library/` data during migration; that is expected.

## Troubleshooting

### Project is locked

Close the graphical Unity editor and ensure no Unity batch process is still running, then rerun the build.

### ADB shows no device

```bash
adb kill-server
adb start-server
adb devices -l
adb connect <PHONE_IP>:5555
adb devices -l
```

Confirm USB debugging or wireless debugging is enabled and that the computer is authorized on the phone. ADB cannot simulate the visual quality of the water; the final touch and tilt check must be performed on the physical device.

### Missing JDK, SDK, or NDK

Check the toolchain paths resolved in `Assets/Editor/BuildAndroidTest.cs`. The API 36 platform should exist at:

```bash
test -f "$LOCALAPPDATA/Unity/AndroidToolchain-6000.6.0f1/SDK/platforms/android-36/android.jar" && echo API_36_OK
```

### Missing package

Read the first package-resolution error in the Unity log. Do not randomly add packages. The known obsolete `com.unity.modules.vr` dependency has already been removed.

### Water looks frozen or black

Check the Unity log for script exceptions. Confirm that `Assets/HelloAndroid.cs` is attached to the object in `Assets/Scenes/HelloAndroid.unity`, and rebuild after clearing a stale `Library/` only as a last resort.

### Food pellets never appear or fish never eat them

- Pellets are only dropped by `TouchPhase.Began` taps and capped at `MaxPellets`; rapid taps beyond the cap are ignored.
- A fish must be **hungry** (cooldown elapsed) and within the chase radius of the pellet to go for it — drop food near a fish and wait a second.
- The runtime log must not show `Can't add component because class 'SphereCollider' doesn't exist` — that means `GameObject.CreatePrimitive` was reintroduced and must be replaced with the hand-built sphere mesh.

### Fish look cut off at the left/right screen edge

This used to happen when a fish swam far from the camera near the border: the placement mapped the fish to the reference plane at `z = 0`, but the perspective frustum is narrower farther away, so the fish fell outside it and looked sliced. All fish placement now goes through `PlaceFishAt`, which scales the mapping with the fish's depth and clamps the body inside the frustum (see the Emperor Angelfish section).

### Fish flicker / synchronized blinking

The fish layer must be treated as one shared render target: a blink affecting every fish at once is not an animation problem. The fix is a disabled fish camera rendered once from `LateUpdate` into a back `RenderTexture`, followed by a front/back reference swap only after rendering completes. `OnGUI` composites only the completed front texture. Do not call `Camera.Render` from `Update`, do not enable the fish camera, and do not reintroduce `Graphics.Blit` between the fish camera and the composited texture.

The animated Emperor renderer also uses `updateWhenOffscreen = true`, while motion vectors, HDR, MSAA, and occlusion culling are disabled for this isolated transparent pass. If flicker returns, first verify `fishRenderTexture` and `fishRenderTextureBack` are distinct and that no other camera targets either texture.

### Fish overlap / slide through each other

`ApplyEmperorSeparation` runs after all fish movement and keeps every fish separated in the composited screen view. It uses projected rectangular body bounds, multiple correction passes, and shallowest-axis resolution so the fish do not overlap without stopping their forward motion. The three companions use independent swim trajectories and vertical bands instead of leader-following convergence, preventing the separation solver from pinning them in place.

### Pause button does not respond to synthetic taps

When scripting taps with `adb`, remember `input tap X Y` uses **top-origin Y** (the pause button is at roughly `X = width - 0.055*width - 26`, `Y = 0.055*width` from the top), while Unity touch coordinates are bottom-origin. Tap the top-right button with a small Y value, not a large one.

### Fish is invisible or misplaced

- Check the runtime log for the `EMPEROR: prefab spawned` and `EMPEROR: calibrated` lines; their absence means the prefab failed to load (missing `Assets/Resources/EmperorAngelfish_swim1.prefab`).
- Verify the materials were converted: the build log must show `converted material to Standard` for both `M_Emperor_body.mat` and `M_Emperor_fins.mat`. A missing conversion leaves the URP Lit shader reference, which renders nothing on this built-in pipeline project.
- The fish renders on layer 8 through its own disabled perspective camera and LateUpdate ping-pong render path; do not change `cullingMask`, `FishLayer`, the front/back texture swap, or the render texture format unless you re-verify on device.
- The centering offset lives on the model in unscaled model units while the fit scale is applied on `fishRoot` (an ancestor). Keep that relationship; applying both on the same transform pushes the fish out of view.

### Water or 3D fish is too slow on a low-end phone

The main water quality/performance controls are `WaterWidth` and `WaterHeight` in `HelloAndroid.cs`. Reduce them proportionally, for example to `88 x 152`, before changing the simulation equations. Keep texture filtering enabled for a smooth result.

The fish uses two `256 x 440` ARGB render textures for ping-pong rendering. If necessary, reduce `fishRenderSize` before simplifying the model; keep the front/back separation intact.

## Working rules for future sessions

1. Read this file before modifying the project.
2. Use Unity 6 `6000.6.0f1` for current Android work.
3. Preserve the portrait lock unless multi-orientation support is a deliberate requirement.
4. Keep the project dependency-free unless a package is genuinely needed.
5. Keep the fish assets in `Resources/` and verify fish/water animation, no-overlap behavior, and continuous motion on the physical device.
6. Use `Assets/Editor/BuildAndroidTest.cs` for repeatable Android builds.
7. Check `adb devices -l` before claiming device verification.
8. Keep generated `Library/`, `Logs/`, `Builds/`, and APK artifacts out of source control (see `.gitignore`).
9. Never place license serials, access tokens, keystore passwords, signing secrets, machine paths, or personal identifiers in this file or in committed code.
10. Before adopting a newer Unity patch, verify Android Build Support, JDK/SDK/NDK compatibility, build success, APK installation, and physical behavior.
11. Keep the pause/ad-spot and food/flee interactions tested on the device: coordinates are mirrored between `adb` (top-origin Y) and Unity `Input.touches` (bottom-origin Y).
